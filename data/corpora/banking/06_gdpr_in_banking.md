# GDPR in Banking

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) governs how organisations in the European Union process personal data. Banks process large amounts of sensitive personal data, so data protection is a core compliance topic alongside financial regulation.

## Lawful Bases and Principles

Every processing activity needs a lawful basis. In banking the most common are performance of a contract (running an account), legal obligation (anti-money laundering checks) and legitimate interests (fraud prevention). Consent is used less often because it can be withdrawn at any time.

Key principles include:

- **Purpose limitation**: data collected for one purpose may not be reused for an incompatible purpose.
- **Data minimisation**: only data that is necessary for the purpose may be collected.
- **Storage limitation**: data must not be kept longer than necessary.
- **Integrity and confidentiality**: appropriate security, such as encryption and access controls.

## Retention Versus Erasure

Customers have a right to erasure, but it is not absolute. When a law requires the bank to keep records, such as the anti-money laundering obligation to keep customer and transaction records for at least five years after the relationship ends, the bank may refuse to delete them until the retention period expires.

## Automated Decisions

Article 22 of the GDPR gives individuals the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that significantly affects them, such as an automatic loan rejection, unless an exception applies. Where such decisions are allowed, the bank must provide safeguards: the right to obtain human intervention, to express a point of view and to contest the decision. Customers are also entitled to meaningful information about the logic involved.

## Data Protection Impact Assessments

Before high-risk processing, for example a new credit scoring model or large-scale profiling, the bank must carry out a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) describing the processing, its necessity, the risks to individuals and the measures to mitigate them.

## Breaches and Penalties

A personal data breach must be reported to the competent supervisory authority within 72 hours of the bank becoming aware of it, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals. Affected customers must be informed when the risk is high. The most serious infringements can lead to fines of up to 20 million euros or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

## Data Protection Officer

Banks appoint a data protection officer who advises on compliance, monitors processing and acts as the contact point for the supervisory authority.
