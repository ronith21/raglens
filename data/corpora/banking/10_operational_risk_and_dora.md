# Operational Risk and DORA

The Basel framework defines operational risk as the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events. The definition includes legal risk but excludes strategic and reputational risk. Examples range from internal fraud and processing errors to cyber attacks, IT outages and natural disasters.

## Managing Operational Risk

Banks identify and assess operational risks through risk and control self-assessments, collect internal loss data, track key risk indicators and run scenario analysis for rare but severe events. Under the revised Basel standards, capital for operational risk is calculated with a standardised approach based on the size of the bank's business and, depending on the jurisdiction, its historical losses.

## The Digital Operational Resilience Act

The Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) is an EU regulation that has applied since 17 January 2025. It creates a single set of rules on information and communication technology (ICT) risk for banks, insurers, investment firms and other financial entities. Its main pillars are:

1. **ICT risk management**: a documented framework, approved by the management body, to identify, protect against, detect, respond to and recover from ICT risks.
2. **ICT incident management and reporting**: major ICT-related incidents must be classified and reported to the competent authority within tight deadlines.
3. **Digital operational resilience testing**: regular testing of systems, and for significant institutions advanced threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) that simulates real attackers.
4. **ICT third-party risk**: contracts with technology providers must contain specific provisions, and firms must keep a register of information on all their ICT service arrangements. Critical ICT third-party providers, such as major cloud providers, are overseen directly by the European supervisory authorities.
5. **Information sharing** on cyber threats between financial entities.

## Business Continuity

Banks maintain business continuity plans that define how critical services are restored after a disruption, including recovery time objectives and alternative sites. Plans are tested regularly, because an untested plan often fails when it is needed.

## Why It Matters

Operational failures can be sudden and visible to customers, for example when a payment system outage prevents salaries from being paid. As banks rely more on a few large technology providers, concentration risk in the supply chain has become a central supervisory concern.
