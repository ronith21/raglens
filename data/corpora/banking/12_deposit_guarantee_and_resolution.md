# Deposit Guarantee Schemes and Bank Resolution

When a bank fails, two sets of rules decide what happens to customers and creditors: deposit guarantee schemes protect ordinary depositors, and the resolution framework determines how the bank itself is restructured or wound down.

## Deposit Guarantee Schemes

Every EU member state must have a deposit guarantee scheme (DGS). Deposits are protected up to 100,000 euros per depositor per bank. The limit applies to the total of a depositor's eligible accounts at the same bank, not to each account separately, so a customer with two accounts of 70,000 euros each at one bank is covered for 100,000 euros in total. Certain temporary high balances, for example from the sale of a private home, can be protected above the standard limit for a limited time.

Schemes must repay depositors quickly; the EU target for payout is seven working days. The schemes are funded by contributions from member banks, calculated partly according to each bank's risk.

## Why Deposit Insurance Matters

Without protection, depositors who fear for a bank have a strong incentive to withdraw their money first, which can turn rumours into an actual bank run. Credible deposit insurance reduces this incentive.

## Bank Resolution

The Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive (BRRD) gives resolution authorities tools to deal with failing banks without using taxpayers' money and without disrupting critical functions such as payments:

- **Sale of business**: selling all or part of the bank to a private buyer.
- **Bridge institution**: transferring critical functions to a temporary bank controlled by the authority.
- **Asset separation**: moving problematic assets into an asset management vehicle.
- **Bail-in**: writing down or converting into equity the claims of shareholders and creditors, in order of seniority, so that they bear the losses.

Covered deposits are excluded from bail-in.

## MREL

To make bail-in credible, banks must hold a minimum requirement for own funds and eligible liabilities (MREL): enough capital and debt that can absorb losses and recapitalise the bank in resolution.

## Who Decides

In the banking union, resolution of significant banks is handled by the Single Resolution Board, supported by the Single Resolution Fund financed by the banking industry. Every bank must also prepare a recovery plan describing how it would restore its own financial position in severe stress.
