# MiFID II and Investor Protection

The Markets in Financial Instruments Directive (MiFID II) and its accompanying regulation set the rules for investment services in the European Union. A central aim is to protect investors when banks sell or recommend financial instruments.

## Client Categorisation

Clients are categorised as **retail clients**, **professional clients** or **eligible counterparties**. Retail clients receive the highest level of protection. Professional clients, such as large companies and institutional investors, are assumed to have more experience, so some protections apply in a lighter form. A retail client can ask to be treated as professional only if specific criteria on experience, trading activity and portfolio size are met.

## Suitability

When a bank provides investment advice or portfolio management, it must carry out a **suitability assessment**. It gathers information on the client's:

- knowledge and experience with the relevant products;
- financial situation, including the ability to bear losses;
- investment objectives, including risk tolerance and investment horizon.

The bank may only recommend products that are suitable for the client and must give the client a suitability report explaining why the recommendation fits the client's profile.

## Appropriateness

For non-advised sales of complex products, the bank performs a lighter **appropriateness test** that checks whether the client has the knowledge and experience to understand the risks. If the product is not appropriate, the bank must warn the client.

## Product Governance

Manufacturers of financial products must define a **target market** of clients for whom each product is designed, and distributors must check that they sell products within that target market.

## Costs and Inducements

Clients must receive clear information on all costs and charges, both before and after the service, so that they can see how costs reduce their returns. Inducements, such as commissions paid by product providers, are restricted and must not harm the quality of the service; for independent advice and portfolio management they are generally banned.

## Record Keeping

Firms must record telephone conversations and electronic communications that relate to client orders, so that the terms of transactions can be verified in case of disputes.
